A library card is one of the best deals around, and most of us use a small part of it. A few habits make it much more useful.

## Place holds early, and freeze them

For a popular new release, get on the hold list as soon as you can. If you're not ready to read it yet, many library systems let you pause (or "freeze") a hold and keep your place in line.

## Read and listen in Libby

Most public libraries, including Boston Public Library, lend ebooks and audiobooks through the Libby app. Ebook and print hold lists are separate, so one is often much shorter than the other. Shelf to Stacks shows both side by side.

## Ask the library to buy it

If a book isn't in the catalog at all, look for a "suggest a purchase" form on your library's website. Libraries use these requests to decide what to order.

## Borrow from other libraries

Interlibrary loan lets your library borrow a book from another library for you. It takes a little longer, but it opens up an enormous catalog. Ask at the desk or look for it on your library's website.

## Look beyond books

- Museum passes and free or discounted admission at many libraries.
- Online learning, newspapers and magazines you'd otherwise pay for.
- Quiet study rooms, events and author talks.

Check your library's website for what's on offer. You might find you've been paying for something you could have borrowed.
